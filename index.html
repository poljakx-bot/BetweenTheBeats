A literary trilogy with an open physics companion

The Logical Cracks in Our Physical Explanations Are Real.

Some of physics’ difficulty belongs to nature. Some appears when explanations hide the steps between an event, the signal that carries evidence of it, the receiver and the conclusion.

Between the Beats follows those missing steps through three novels—from cosmology and relativity to light, matter and detection.

The measurements remain. The mechanisms used to explain them are examined again.

No equations. Not even E=mc2. The technical material appears only in the companion, when you choose to go deeper.

The trilogy

Three books, three questions

The trilogy keeps the measurements in place and asks whether their physical explanations could be different.

Novel One

Between the Beats

Publication scheduled · September 3, 2026

The first book explores cosmology, the propagation of light and the apparent expansion of the universe. It asks whether the observed effects can be explained differently while preserving the measurements already made.

Central question: Can the same cosmological observations support a different explanation?

Novel Two

Speed Without a Message

Complete manuscript · planned autumn 2026

The second book examines relative motion, the observation of light and the difference between an event and the signal that reports it. It asks whether Einstein solved the wrong physical problem.

Central question: Did Einstein solve the wrong problem?

Novel Three

Titraj

Complete manuscript · planned early 2027

The third book explores how electromagnetic waves are created and what physically happens when matter detects them. It asks whether the photon is necessary as the physical explanation of the measured phenomena.

Central question: Do the measured phenomena require the photon as their physical explanation?

The question between the books and the physics

What if some of physics’ hardest paradoxes began when we confused an event with the signal that tells us it happened?
Event→Signal→Receiver→Judgment

The portal does not ask you to accept a finished replacement theory. It asks you to keep the physical stages separate long enough to inspect the argument for yourself.

The second passenger in the train

The familiar train argument focuses on the passenger at the midpoint. Adding another passenger reveals something more basic: simultaneous reception belongs to the local meeting place of the two flashes, not automatically to an entire reference frame.
