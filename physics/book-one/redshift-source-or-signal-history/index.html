Redshift: Source History or Signal History?

The shift is measured. The history assigned to it is reconstructed.

Published essay

Four questions to keep separate

Measurement is not the same thing as its explanation.

As you read, keep four tasks distinct: what is measured; what transformation the signal displays; what physical mechanism is assigned to that transformation; and what observation could distinguish competing mechanisms.

Intuition

A shifted spectrum tells us what arrived

Redshift is real. A known pattern of spectral features is received at longer wavelengths and lower frequencies than its laboratory reference. The observation establishes a scaling of the received spectrum. It does not carry its own causal label.

Redshift is measured in the signal. Its physical history is reconstructed.

The path is not empty

Light travelling across astronomical distances does not pass through a mathematical nothing. Ionised matter means free electrons and ions: a plasma. Plasma responds to electromagnetic fields, changes their propagation, and can exchange energy with them. High-frequency light interacting with charged particles can also emerge at a changed frequency; Compton scattering is a direct laboratory example of that possibility.

This does not establish that cosmological redshift is a plasma effect. It removes a weaker assumption: that the propagation channel must be electromagnetically passive.

One transformation can leave several traces

A signal contains more than spectral lines. It has envelopes, durations, correlations and structure in time. If one transformation rescales the signal time axis, then a lower carrier frequency and a longer observed duration can be two consequences of the same transformation rather than two independent demonstrations of its cause.

The Argument

1. What is measured

For an identified spectral feature, redshift is defined by

\[z=\frac{\lambda_{obs}-\lambda_0}{\lambda_0},\qquad 1+z=\frac{\lambda_{obs}}{\lambda_0}=\frac{\nu_0}{\nu_{obs}}.\]

The detector supplies the received spectrum. Laboratory spectroscopy supplies the reference pattern. The measured result is a relationship between the two.

2. What a full signal transformation looks like

If the complete waveform is uniformly stretched, one may write schematically

\[x_{obs}(t)=A(z)\,x_{emit}\!\left(\frac{t-t_0}{1+z}\right).\]

The same transformation gives

\[\nu_{obs}=\frac{\nu_{emit}}{1+z},\qquad \Delta t_{obs}=(1+z)\Delta t_{emit}.\]

These are two signatures of one mathematical rescaling. Observing both constrains the transformation strongly, but it does not by itself identify the physical mechanism that produced it.

3. Treat propagation as a communication channel

Write the received signal schematically as

\[S_{obs}=\mathcal D\!\left[\mathcal C_L(S_{emit})\right],\]

where \(\mathcal C_L\) is the propagation channel accumulated over distance \(L\), and \(\mathcal D\) is detection. If the channel were perfectly passive, it would not create a systematic spectral drift. Real ionised space is not perfectly passive: free charges respond to the field, plasma is dispersive, and energy exchange between radiation and matter is physically allowed.

4. Frequency-changing interaction is not hypothetical

A light–charge interaction can change the outgoing radiation frequency while transferring energy and momentum to matter. Compton scattering is the clearest high-frequency example. It is used here only to establish the physical possibility of frequency-changing electromagnetic interaction with charge, not as a claim that rare ordinary Compton scattering explains astronomical redshift.

5. From microscopic interactions to a channel law

Let \(R(\nu)\) be the interaction rate per unit path and let \(\langle\delta\nu\rangle_\nu\) be the mean frequency change produced by the local physics. The macroscopic drift is then

\[A(\nu)=R(\nu)\,\langle\delta\nu\rangle_\nu,\qquad \frac{d\langle\nu\rangle}{dx}=A(\nu).\]

Statistics does not create the frequency dependence. The dependence on \(\nu\) must come from the interaction itself. Statistics determines how the mean and the spread accumulate.

6. What homogeneity contributes

If the plasma is statistically similar over many successive regions, the same local bias can keep the same sign over a very long path. Random deviations need not vanish, but they need not reverse the mean drift. A useful coarse-grained description is

\[d\nu=A(\nu)\,dx+\sqrt{2D(\nu)}\,dW_x.\]

Here \(A\) is systematic drift and \(D\) describes spectral diffusion or broadening. Homogeneity does not create \(A(\nu)\); it allows a local law to accumulate coherently in the statistical sense.

7. The required redshift law

For an approximately multiplicative redshift, the channel would need a regime close to

\[A(\nu)\simeq-\beta\nu.\]

Then

\[\nu(L)=\nu_0e^{-\beta L},\qquad 1+z=e^{\beta L}.\]

This is the mathematical target, not yet a derived plasma law. The central physical problem is to obtain the sign, magnitude and frequency dependence of \(A(\nu)\) from the actual plasma interaction while preserving the information in the signal.

Deep Notes

Start from the observation

Redshift is determined by comparing a received spectral pattern with a laboratory reference. For a feature with rest wavelength \(\lambda_0\) observed at \(\lambda_{obs}\),

\[1+z=\frac{\lambda_{obs}}{\lambda_0}=\frac{\nu_0}{\nu_{obs}}.\]

A positive \(z\) means that the received pattern lies at longer wavelength and lower frequency. This equation records the transformation between reference and observation. It does not contain a microscopic mechanism.

Why the whole waveform matters

Suppose the emitted signal is \(x_{emit}(t)\). A uniform time-axis rescaling can be written as

\[x_{obs}(t)=A\,x_{emit}\!\left(\frac{t-t_0}{1+z}\right).\]

Fourier scaling then moves every frequency by the reciprocal factor while characteristic time intervals grow by \(1+z\):

\[\nu_{obs}=\frac{\nu_{emit}}{1+z},\qquad \Delta t_{obs}=(1+z)\Delta t_{emit}.\]

The frequency shift and the duration change are therefore mathematically linked if both arise from one common rescaling. They may provide two observables of that transformation without being independent evidence for the physical cause of the transformation.

The propagation channel is a physical system

Remote astronomy can be written as a forward problem:

\[S_{obs}=\mathcal D\!\left[\mathcal C_L(S_{emit})\right].\]

The operator \(\mathcal C_L\) represents everything that happens to the electromagnetic field between emission and detection. Intergalactic and circumgalactic space contain ionised matter. Free electrons and ions respond to electromagnetic fields, so the channel can possess dispersion, fluctuations and dissipative energy exchange. The statement “space is dilute” is not equivalent to “the channel is physically inert”.

Interaction, dissipation and thermalisation

When an electromagnetic field drives charges, the response can be purely reactive in an ideal lossless limit: energy is borrowed from the field and returned. Real plasma also has channels through which organised field energy becomes particle energy and can later be redistributed or thermalised.

Schematically:

electromagnetic field ↓ charge response ↓ energy / momentum exchange ↓ particle energisation ↓ redistribution and thermalisation

No additional material beyond the plasma is required in principle for dissipation. Other matter can contribute, but the free charges themselves already provide dynamical degrees of freedom.

Why dissipation alone is not enough

A conventional attenuation law

\[\frac{dI}{dx}=-\alpha I\]

reduces intensity. It does not automatically imply

\[\frac{d\nu}{dx}<0.\]

The missing step is therefore not “where can the energy go?” Plasma provides an answer to that. The missing step is “under what interaction does the outgoing coherent signal acquire a systematic frequency drift rather than only a lower amplitude?”

A known proof of principle: light plus charge can change frequency

Compton scattering provides a clean example. High-frequency radiation interacts with an electron; the outgoing radiation can have a longer wavelength while the electron receives recoil energy and momentum. In the conventional bookkeeping,

\[\Delta\lambda=\frac{h}{m_ec}(1-\cos\theta).\]

The important point here is not to promote ordinary Compton scattering into a cosmological mechanism. It is that a frequency-changing interaction between electromagnetic radiation and charge is experimentally real. The channel question is whether a much weaker collective or cumulative plasma process can produce an ordered drift over astronomical distance.

Separate microphysics from statistics

Let a local interaction at frequency \(\nu\) occur with rate \(R(\nu)\) per unit path. Let the distribution of frequency changes in one interaction be \(p(\delta\nu\mid\nu)\). Its first two moments are

\[\mu(\nu)=\langle\delta\nu\rangle_\nu,\qquad \sigma^2(\nu)=\langle(\delta\nu-\mu)^2\rangle_\nu.\]

The coarse-grained coefficients are then of the form

\[A(\nu)=R(\nu)\mu(\nu),\qquad D(\nu)\sim\frac12R(\nu)\sigma^2(\nu).\]

This makes the roles explicit. The microscopic interaction determines the sign and frequency dependence of the drift. Statistics determines the accumulation of the mean and the spread. It cannot manufacture the required \(\nu\)-dependence if the local physics does not contain it.

What homogeneity does — and does not do

A relatively homogeneous channel means that the local distribution of plasma properties does not change arbitrarily from one small region to the next. If the same interaction produces a small negative mean bias repeatedly, the mean can accumulate approximately linearly with the number of effective steps, while uncorrelated fluctuations grow more slowly in relative importance.

Homogeneity does not choose red rather than blue. That sign must come from energy exchange and plasma dynamics. Its role is to make a local statistical law capable of producing a stable macroscopic trend instead of a sequence of unrelated regional effects.

The target for a multiplicative redshift

A universal fractional loss law would have the form

\[\frac{1}{\nu}\frac{d\nu}{dx}\simeq-\beta.\]

Integration gives

\[\nu(L)=\nu_0e^{-\beta L}\]

and therefore

\[1+z=\frac{\nu_0}{\nu(L)}=e^{\beta L}.\]

This is attractive because every spectral component experiences the same fractional scaling, preserving relative spectral structure. But it must emerge from the plasma microphysics. Assuming it in advance would only rename the observation.

Broadening is a second prediction, not an afterthought

The same interaction that produces a mean drift also produces a distribution around that mean. A channel model therefore predicts both

\[A(\nu)\]

and

\[D(\nu).\]

Narrow astronomical spectral features place a direct constraint on their ratio. The requirement is not that \(D\) vanish, but that the accumulated diffusion remain compatible with the observed preservation of spectral information while the mean drift grows to the required size.

Energy deposited in matter need not accumulate without limit

If radiation transfers energy to plasma, the thermal history of that matter is an energy-balance problem rather than an unlimited one-way temperature rise. A fluid element can be represented schematically by

\[\frac{dU}{dt}=\dot Q_{abs}-P\frac{dV}{dt}-\dot Q_{rad}+\cdots.\]

Heating from electromagnetic interaction can be offset by expansion work, radiation and other cooling processes. Expansion cools; compression heats. This removes the naive objection that any cumulative transfer from light to matter would require space to become arbitrarily hot. It does not determine whether the transfer is large enough to explain redshift.

What is established and what remains open

It is established that ionised matter interacts with electromagnetic fields, that plasma changes propagation, that radiation can transfer energy to charged matter, and that light–charge interactions can change the frequency of outgoing radiation. None of those facts by itself demonstrates a cosmological redshift mechanism.

The open question is narrower and quantitative:

Can a weak, cumulative interaction with a statistically homogeneous cosmic plasma produce a stable negative fractional frequency drift while preserving the signal’s spectral, temporal and angular information?

Open obligation

A channel explanation must derive, from actual plasma parameters rather than from the observed redshift, the functions \(A(\nu)\) and \(D(\nu)\). It must obtain the required sign and approximately multiplicative frequency law, the observed magnitude over astronomical distance, acceptable line broadening, and the associated energy and momentum transfer.

If the microphysics instead predicts a strongly chromatic law, excessive scattering, excessive broadening, or an effect many orders of magnitude too small, the proposed channel mechanism fails. The point of the channel formulation is that it converts the idea into those quantitative tests.
