Redshift: Source History or Signal History?

The shift is measured. The physical story assigned to the shift is not written into the ratio itself.

Published essay

The question

What does the measured redshift establish before a cosmological history is assigned to it?

The received spectrum gives a ratio. Turning that ratio into motion, geometry, gravity or propagation history is a second physical step.

Intuition

A shifted spectrum does not carry a label saying why it shifted

A known spectral pattern is received at longer wavelengths and lower frequencies. That shift is real and measurable.

\[1+z=\frac{\lambda_{obs}}{\lambda_0}=\frac{f_0}{f_{obs}}.\]

The ratio tells us how the received spectrum is displaced relative to the laboratory reference.

It does not contain, inside the number itself, a label saying “expansion”, “motion”, “gravity” or “propagation history”.

Redshift is measured. Its physical cause is inferred.

Why this matters

Once one cause is adopted, the same redshift can be used to infer distance, lookback time and source history. Those inferences can be extremely successful, but they remain downstream of the physical interpretation assigned to the measured shift.

The Argument

1. What the spectrum gives

Identify a spectral feature whose laboratory wavelength is \(\lambda_0\). The detector records it at \(\lambda_{obs}\). The redshift is

\[z=\frac{\lambda_{obs}-\lambda_0}{\lambda_0}.\]

Several matching features shifted by the same factor make the identification strong.

2. What the ratio does not contain

The measured ratio is a relation between an observed spectral scale and a reference spectral scale. It does not, by itself, specify the physical history that produced the difference.

3. The standard cosmological interpretation

In the expanding-universe description,

\[1+z=\frac{a(t_{obs})}{a(t_{emit})}.\]

Within that framework, redshift becomes part of a larger connected account of distance, age and cosmic history.

The equation is therefore not the raw measurement. It is the physical model connecting the raw ratio to cosmic scale factor.

4. The portal’s alternative question

Path I asks whether part of what is normally assigned to source history could instead belong to signal history during propagation.

That question does not change the measured redshift. It changes which physical stage is being asked to explain it.

5. The conclusion

\[\boxed{\text{measured redshift}\neq\text{measured physical cause of redshift}.}\]

The next theme asks what Type Ia supernova duration adds once the signal is known to be redshifted.

Deep Notes

Begin with a received spectrum. A detector records spectral features at observed wavelengths or frequencies. Redshift is the measured displacement of an identified pattern relative to a laboratory reference; the physical cause of that displacement is a separate question.

1. From a received feature to a dimensionless ratio

Suppose a spectral feature is observed at wavelength \(\lambda_{obs}\) and identified with a laboratory feature at \(\lambda_0\). The redshift is defined by

\[z=\frac{\lambda_{obs}-\lambda_0}{\lambda_0},\]

so

\[1+z=\frac{\lambda_{obs}}{\lambda_0}.\]

Using \(f=c/\lambda\), the same scaling can be written

\[1+z=\frac{f_0}{f_{obs}}.\]

The directly received quantities are the observed spectral positions. The laboratory reference and the identification connect those observations to a known transition.

2. What the ratio establishes

If several recognised features are all displaced by the same factor, the evidence for an ordered spectral scaling becomes strong. What has been established is the relation between the received spectrum and the reference spectrum.

The number \(z\) itself does not specify which physical process created that relation. Relative motion, gravity and cosmological geometry are examples of physical settings in which frequency shifts are described; a proposed propagation effect would be another type of explanation. The measured ratio is prior to choosing among such causes.

3. The standard cosmological map

In the expanding-universe description, the measured redshift is connected to the scale factor by

\[1+z=\frac{a(t_{obs})}{a(t_{emit})}.\]

This equation gives the measured ratio a cosmological history. Once embedded in the wider model, redshift can be related to emission epoch, distance and other reconstructed source properties.

That wider explanatory success is distinct from the narrower logical point: the scale-factor relation is the physical interpretation of the measured spectral ratio, not an additional quantity read directly from the detector.

4. What a propagation interpretation would have to mean

If part of the shift were assigned to signal history during travel, the measured value of \(z\) would not change. What changes is the proposed location of the physical process: instead of assigning the entire scaling to source–observer geometry, some transformation would be assigned to propagation.

That idea is not a mechanism by itself. A physical propagation account would still have to explain why recognised spectral structure remains ordered and why the rest of the received signal behaves as observed.

5. The conclusion

\[\boxed{\text{spectral displacement is observed; its physical history is inferred}.}\]

This page stops at that distinction. It does not require a propagation alternative to be true in order for the distinction itself to hold.
