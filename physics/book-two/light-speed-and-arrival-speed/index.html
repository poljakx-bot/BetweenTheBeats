Light Speed Is Not Arrival Speed

A travelling light front may propagate at c while its distance from a moving receiver closes at c±v. These are different physical rates.

Published essay

Measurement is not the same thing as its explanation.

Intuition

A light signal and its arrival at a particular receiver are not the same physical quantity.

The speed of light describes how a light front, pulse edge or identifiable mark propagates. The speed of arrival describes how quickly that travelling feature and a chosen receiver approach their meeting event.

\[\boxed{\text{light speed} \neq \text{arrival speed}}\]

A receiver moving towards a front meets it sooner. A receiver moving away meets it later. The motion changes the rate at which the remaining separation closes; it does not require the electromagnetic front itself to acquire the receiver’s velocity.

This distinction also protects a more basic one. The source event does not travel. What travels is the electromagnetic consequence that carries information about it.

The Argument

Let a marked light front move to the right in an external coordinate system:

\[x_F(t)=x_0+ct.\]

Its propagation speed in that system is therefore

\[\frac{dx_F}{dt}=c.\]

Let a receiver move along the same line:

\[x_R(t)=x_{R0}+vt.\]

The front arrives when both trajectories reach the same event:

\[x_F(t_{\rm rec})=x_R(t_{\rm rec}).\]

The separation between them is

\[D(t)=x_R(t)-x_F(t),\]

so its rate of change is

\[\frac{dD}{dt}=v-c.\]

For a receiver moving away in the direction of the front, the magnitude of the closing rate is

\[v_{\rm arrival}=c-v.\]

For a receiver moving towards an incoming front, the separation closes at

\[v_{\rm arrival}=c+v.\]

The two expressions answer different questions:

\[\boxed{v_{\rm propagation}=c},\qquad \boxed{v_{\rm arrival}=c\pm v}.\]

The first follows the light feature through the selected coordinate system. The second follows the changing distance between that feature and a particular receiver.

A marked front is not the event

A lightning strike, switch closure or clock mark occurs at the source. It may create a distinguishable front, pulse edge or change inside a longer signal. The receiver later meets that travelling record.

\[\text{source event}\rightarrow\text{travelling record}\rightarrow\text{arrival event}.\]

Those three stages are causally connected, but they are not one event moving through space.

Deep Notes

One trajectory, two rates

The same light-front trajectory can be used in two calculations. Differentiating the front position gives its propagation speed. Differentiating its separation from a moving receiver gives the arrival or closing speed. No contradiction exists because the calculations refer to different pairs of events.

Why an arrival rate may exceed (c)

If a receiver and a front approach one another, their separation may close at (c+v). Nothing travels from one to the other at that sum. Two already existing trajectories converge on one local meeting event. The sum is therefore not a superluminal signal speed.

A sequence of marks

Suppose two marks are emitted a time (T) apart. In the source description their separation after emission is

\[\Delta x=cT.\]

A receiver moving towards the sequence meets them at intervals

\[\Delta t_{\rm rec}=\frac{\Delta x}{c+v}=\frac{T}{1+v/c}.\]

A receding receiver meets them at

\[\Delta t_{\rm rec}=\frac{\Delta x}{c-v}=\frac{T}{1-v/c}.\]

The altered cadence belongs directly to reception. It does not return to the completed source history and alter the interval at which the marks were produced.

Retarded time keeps the histories separate

The field received now represents an earlier source state. For a fixed propagation distance (R),

\[t_{\rm ret}=t_{\rm rec}-\frac{R}{c}.\]

This equation identifies which earlier source time is carried in the arriving field. It does not say that the source clock ran slowly; it says that information required time to propagate.

Canonical terminology for Path II

  • Propagation speed follows the travelling wave feature.
  • Arrival speed or closing rate follows the separation between that feature and a receiver.
  • Reception event is their local meeting.
  • Source event remains part of the source history.

What this theme establishes

Motion of the receiver changes arrival without requiring a change in the propagation speed of the light front. Any later argument about clocks or simultaneity must keep those quantities separate.

Further reading

Connection to the novel

Jan’s correction begins with vocabulary. A signal can travel, an event cannot, and the rate at which a receiver meets the signal is not automatically the speed of the signal itself.

The distinction becomes the foundation for every later question about clocks, frames and reports.
